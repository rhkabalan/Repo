# ATHAR — Location research

Working document for the historical spine of the game. **Shia sources only.** Each location
records what the sources support and, separately, the fictional gameplay built around it.
**Nothing in the "Fictional gameplay" rows is historical evidence.**

Status key: ✅ located in the cited work (page or number recorded) · 🟡 cited by a Shia
secondary work or index; the primary passage still has to be read and recorded · ⛔ not
supported / do not depict.

## Locations

### 1. Mecca — arrival and Ali's hospitality (playable now)

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | Abu Dharr hears of the Prophet, comes to Mecca, and accepts Islam; Ali hosts the stranger. |
| Source and passage | 🟡 Bihar vol. 22 (from al-Saduq's *Amali*, Imam al-Sadiq): his journey to Mecca and acceptance. Great Companion retells Ali's hospitality. Record the exact pages and compare the two accounts before scripting dialogue. |
| Date | Early Meccan period, c. 610–613 CE. No exact year in these sources. Range. |
| Evidence of role | Seeker and guest; no fighting. |
| Fictional gameplay | Staff training; market district with supplies and people to help; **repairing townspeople's houses**; crate puzzle; extortioners blocking the plaza (fight / sneak / side door). All of it belongs to the fictional traveler. |

### 2. Among Ghifar — return to his people

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | His return to Ghifar and teaching them; the tribe accepts Islam. |
| Source and passage | 🟡 Great Companion, ch. 3. |
| Date | Between c. 611 and 622 CE. Range. |
| Evidence of role | Teaching his community; no combat. |
| Fictional gameplay | **Building the camp**: pitching tents, digging and lining wells, pens for the herds; carrying water to travelers. |

### 3. Medina

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | His move to Medina; the Prophet's praise of his truthfulness. |
| Source and passage | 🟡 Great Companion, ch. 4. ✅ Ma'ani al-Akhbar: Imam al-Sadiq confirms "ما أظلّت الخضراء ولا أقلّت الغبراء على ذي لهجةٍ أصدق من أبي ذر". Also al-Kashshi. |
| Date | After 622 CE; ch. 4 implies after the Trench (627). 🟡 Range 622–628. |
| Note on campaigns | Ch. 4: he had **not** joined Badr, Uhud or the Trench. ⛔ Never show Abu Dharr there. |
| Fictional gameplay | **Building the Prophet's Mosque** with the companions (mud bricks, palm-trunk pillars, palm-frond roof). **Badr (624)** and **digging and defending the Trench (627)** are played by the fictional character only — non-graphic: opponents fall back, flee or surrender. Imam Ali's deeds there are told in narration from al-Irshad. |

### 4. Road to Tabuk

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | The Tabuk expedition; Abu Dharr's camel fails, he carries his baggage on his back and follows on foot; the Prophet's words "he walks alone, dies alone, and will be raised alone". |
| Source and passage | ✅ Bihar vol. 22 p. 433 (from al-Rawandi, *al-Khara'ij*, from Imam al-Sadiq). The Message ch. 53. Hayat al-Qulub vol. 2 (water account; compare variants). |
| Date | 9 AH, 630 CE. |
| Evidence of role | Traveler. **No battle took place at Tabuk.** ⛔ No Roman battle. |
| Fictional gameplay | Survival: water, stamina, sandstorms, route-finding. |

### 5. Sham and Jabal Amil — spreading Islam

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | In Sham he speaks against hoarding wealth (Qur'an 9:34) and refuses Mu'awiya's gifts; tradition holds that he taught in Jabal Amil (Sarafand on the coast, Mays al-Jabal in the hills), then returned to Damascus. |
| Source and passage | 🟡 Great Companion ch. 14; al-Ya'qubi; al-Mas'udi; A'yan al-Shi'a vol. 4. |
| Date | Caliphate of 'Uthman, c. 645–651 CE. Range. |
| Evidence of presence | Sham: reported in al-Ya'qubi and al-Mas'udi. Jabal Amil: reported tradition with shrines at Sarafand and Mays al-Jabal — label "reported tradition". |
| Evidence of role | Teaching; defending the poor's due. Words only. |
| Route used in the game | Medina → Damascus → (coast) Sarafand → Mays al-Jabal → Damascus → Medina → al-Rabadhah. The order of the Jabal Amil stops is the game's assumption, not a sourced itinerary. |
| Fictional gameplay | Travel map between the stops; at each village help people (water, harvest, repairing terraces) and carry his teaching to them through conversation; market debates about wealth. |

### 6. Al-Rabadhah — farewell and death

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | Sent from Medina to al-Rabadhah. Imam Ali, al-Hasan, al-Husayn, 'Aqil, 'Abdullah ibn Ja'far and 'Ammar walk out to bid him farewell. He dies at al-Rabadhah; passing travelers, among them Malik al-Ashtar, shroud and bury him. |
| Source and passage | ✅ al-Kafi vol. 8 p. 207 no. 251; ✅ al-Faqih no. 2428; Nahj al-Balagha Sermon 130. 🟡 al-Kashshi for the burial by Malik al-Ashtar. |
| Date | Death 32 AH / 652–653 CE (some give 31 AH). |
| Fictional gameplay | Quiet chapter: caretaking, the final journal. |

## Period look (for art and level design)

| Place | Show | Do not show (later than the events) |
|---|---|---|
| Mecca c. 610–620 | Barren granite valley, no palms. Kaaba as rebuilt by Quraysh c. 605: courses of stone and wood, raised door, cloth cover. Houses around an open court; Zamzam. | Black kiswa with gold band, colonnades, minarets, domes, marble. |
| Ghifar | Black goat-hair tents, herds, wells, acacia, the caravan road. | Permanent towns. |
| Medina c. 622–632 | Palm groves, wells, clan towers, lava fields, Uhud ridge. The Prophet's mosque of mud brick, palm-trunk pillars, palm-frond roof. | Any dome or minaret. |
| Badr | Wells in a sandy plain between low hills. | Banners with later symbols; blood. |
| Road to Tabuk | Sandstone mesas, basalt hills, heat, scarce wells. | Any battle. |
| Damascus c. 645–651 | Roman street grid, colonnaded street, the Church of St John precinct, orchards. | The Umayyad Mosque (built from 705). |
| Jabal Amil | Olive and vine terraces, limestone villages, coastal Sarafand. | Crusader castles. |
| al-Rabadhah | Open grazing plain, wells, few dwellings. | Later town. |
