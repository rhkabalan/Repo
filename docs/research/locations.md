# ATHAR — Location research

Working document for the historical spine of the game. Each location records what the
sources support and, separately, the fictional gameplay built around it. **Nothing in the
"Fictional gameplay" column is historical evidence.**

Status key: ✅ checked against the cited passage · 🟡 lead from a secondary source, needs
the primary references · ⛔ not supported / do not depict.

## Sources in use

| Short name | Work | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Great Companion | *The Great Companion of the Prophet — Abu Dharr*, Islamic Seminary Publications (PDF/EPUB on the Shia digital library) | A later compilation. Use it to organise the storyline; check its narrative details and dialogue against the references it cites. |
| The Message | Jaʿfar Subhani, *The Message*, ch. 53 "The Battle of Tabuk" | Abu Dharr's journey after his camel failed; the army reached Tabuk without meeting a Roman army. |
| Hayat al-Qulub | Muhammad Baqir al-Majlisi, *Hayat al-Qulub*, vol. 2 (Hunayn through Tabuk) | Contains a version of the Tabuk water account. Inclusion in the collection is not verification; compare variants and trace the cited reports. |
| Bukhari | *Sahih al-Bukhari* 3861 (Ibn ʿAbbas) and 3522; 1406 and 4660 (Zayd ibn Wahb) | Conversion in Mecca, Ali's hospitality, the beating at the Kaaba and al-ʿAbbas's rescue; the dispute in Sham over Qur'an 9:34 and the move to al-Rabadhah. |
| Muslim | *Sahih Muslim* 2473–2474 | Abu Dharr's own account: his brother Unays, the stay in Mecca, Zamzam, and Ghifar's acceptance of Islam (half before the Prophet reached Medina, half after). |
| Ibn Hisham | *al-Sira al-Nabawiyya* (Ibn Ishaq), "The affair of Abu Dharr" (Tabuk) | The camel that slowed him, walking alone, "May God have mercy on Abu Dharr: he walks alone, dies alone, and will be raised alone", and Ibn Masʿud recalling it at al-Rabadhah. |
| Tirmidhi | *Jamiʿ al-Tirmidhi* 3801 | "There is none under the sky or on the earth more truthful than Abu Dharr" (graded hasan/sahih). |
| Nahj al-Balagha | Sermon 130 | Imam Ali's address to Abu Dharr on his exile to al-Rabadhah. Keep the sermon separate from the page's later explanatory footnote; parallel references are given in al-Kulayni, al-Yaʿqubi and others. |

## Locations

### 1. Mecca — arrival and Ali's hospitality (playable now)

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | Abu Dharr comes to Mecca seeking the Prophet; Ali hosts the stranger and later guides him. |
| Source and passage | ✅ Bukhari 3861 / 3522; Muslim 2473–2474. His brother Unays goes ahead; Abu Dharr comes himself, stays near the Kaaba living on Zamzam water, Ali notices the stranger and hosts him, then leads him to the Prophet. He declares his faith publicly at the Kaaba, is beaten, and al-ʿAbbas saves him by reminding Quraysh that their trade road to Sham passes through Ghifar. It happens twice. Great Companion retells the same events. |
| Date | Early Meccan period, c. 610–613 CE. No exact year in these sources; reports call him among the first few to accept Islam (the count varies). Show as a range. |
| Evidence of presence | ✅ First-person narration in Muslim 2473; Ibn ʿAbbas narration in Bukhari 3861. |
| Evidence of role | Seeker and guest; no fighting. |
| Fictional gameplay | Staff training in a yard; market district with supplies and people to help; crate puzzle; extortioners blocking the plaza (fight / sneak / side door). All of it belongs to the fictional traveler. |

### 2. Among Ghifar — return to his people (next chapter)

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | His return from Mecca to his people and teaching among Ghifar. |
| Source and passage | ✅ Muslim 2473: told to return to his people; Unays and their mother accept Islam; half of Ghifar accept, the rest when the Prophet reaches Medina; Aslam follow. Great Companion ch. 3. |
| Date | Between his acceptance (c. 610–613) and the Hijra (622 CE); exact years not given. Range. |
| Evidence of presence | ✅ First-person narration (Muslim 2473). |
| Evidence of role | Teaching his community; no combat. |
| Fictional gameplay | Homecoming journey; community tasks (water, provisions); helping travelers on the route; route-finding between camps. |

### 3. Medina

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | His move to Medina. |
| Source and passage | Great Companion, ch. 4. Muslim 2473 places the rest of Ghifar's acceptance at the Prophet's arrival in Medina. 🟡 Exact date of his own move. |
| Date | After 622 CE; ch. 4 implies after the Trench (627). 🟡 Range: 622–628. |
| Evidence of presence | Reported in ch. 4; Tirmidhi 3801 records the Prophet's praise of his truthfulness. |
| Evidence of role | 🟡 To be detailed. |
| Note on campaigns | Ch. 4 says he had **not** joined Badr, Uhud or the Trench. ⛔ Do not depict him fighting there. Cross-check before finalising the chronology. |
| Fictional gameplay | City exploration, community questions and debates, preparation for the road. |

### 4. Road to Tabuk (survival chapter)

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | The Tabuk expedition; Abu Dharr falls behind when his camel can no longer continue, and follows on foot. |
| Source and passage | ✅ Ibn Hisham, "The affair of Abu Dharr": his camel slows him, he carries his baggage on his back and follows on foot; the Prophet says "He walks alone, dies alone, and will be raised alone." The Message, ch. 53; Hayat al-Qulub vol. 2 (water account variant). |
| Date | Rajab 9 AH, autumn 630 CE (standard chronology). |
| Evidence of presence | Participation in the expedition reported in The Message, ch. 53. |
| Evidence of role | Traveler on the expedition. **No battle took place at Tabuk.** ⛔ No Roman battle may be depicted. |
| Fictional gameplay | Survival and endurance: carrying supplies, managing water and stamina, sandstorms, route-finding, reaching the camp. Fictional hazards only (terrain, weather, wildlife). |

### 5. Al-Sham and Jabal Amil

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | His activities in Syria and Jabal Amil, then his return to Syria. |
| Source and passage | ✅ Bukhari 1406 / 4660 (Zayd ibn Wahb, told by Abu Dharr at al-Rabadhah): in Sham he disputed with Muʿawiya over Qur'an 9:34 (those who hoard gold and silver). Muʿawiya held it referred to the People of the Book; Abu Dharr held it covered Muslims too. Muʿawiya wrote to ʿUthman, who called him back to Medina. Jabal Amil: Great Companion ch. 14 (Sarafand, Mays al-Jabal). |
| Date | Caliphate of ʿUthman, c. 645–651 CE. 🟡 Range. |
| Evidence of presence | Sham ✅ (Bukhari). Jabal Amil: reported tradition with shrines at Sarafand and Mays al-Jabal; not found in the Sunni hadith collections checked — "reported tradition, requires corroboration". |
| Evidence of role | Speaking against hoarding wealth; defending the poor's due. Verbal only. |
| Limits | This chapter alone does not establish an exact itinerary, dates or building layouts. Do not invent them. |
| Fictional gameplay | Market districts, verbal debates about wealth, protecting the poor from extortion (fictional, non-graphic). |

### 6. Al-Rabadhah — farewell

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Event | Exile to al-Rabadhah; Imam Ali's farewell address. |
| Source and passage | Nahj al-Balagha, Sermon 130 (sermon text only, not the footnote). Bukhari 1406: he settled at al-Rabadhah after returning to Medina. Whether it was an exile or his own choice is disputed between sources — show both. Ibn Hisham: Ibn Masʿud, passing with travelers from Iraq, finds him and recalls the Tabuk saying. |
| Date | Death 32 AH / 652–653 CE (some give 31 AH). |
| Evidence of presence | ✅ Bukhari 1406 (Zayd ibn Wahb met him there). |
| Evidence of role | Exile; themes of truthfulness, faith and resisting worldly pressure. |
| Fictional gameplay | Quiet, reflective chapter: caretaking tasks, the final journal. |

## Period look (for art and level design)

| Place | Show | Do not show (later than the events) |
|---|---|---|
| Mecca c. 610–620 | Barren granite valley, no palms (cf. Qur'an 14:37). Kaaba as rebuilt by Quraysh c. 605: alternating stone and wood courses, raised door, cloth cover. Houses crowding an open court; Zamzam well. | Black kiswa with gold band (Abbasid and later), colonnades, minarets, domes, marble, the Masjid al-Haram. |
| Ghifar | Black goat-hair tents, herds, wells, acacia, the caravan road between Mecca and Sham. | Permanent towns. |
| Medina c. 622–632 | Palm groves, wells, clan towers (*atam*), lava fields, Uhud ridge. The Prophet's mosque: mud brick, palm-trunk pillars, palm-frond roof. | The green dome (19th c.), any dome or minaret. |
| Road to Tabuk | Sandstone mesas, black basalt hills, heat, scarce wells; the army passed al-Hijr. | Any battle with the Romans. |
| Damascus c. 645–651 | Roman street grid, colonnaded Straight Street, the Church of St John precinct, Ghouta orchards. | The Umayyad Mosque and its mosaics (built from 705). |
| Jabal Amil | Olive and vine terraces, limestone villages, coastal Sarepta. | Crusader castles (12th c.). |
| al-Rabadhah | Open grazing plain east of Medina, wells, few dwellings. | The later Abbasid pilgrim-road town. |
